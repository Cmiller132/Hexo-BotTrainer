# MCTS resolution test: 512 vs 1024 visits (2026-06-01)

**Question:** does doubling the search budget change the search's *decisions*, or has
512 already converged?

**Method.** Frozen `epoch_000020.pt` (96ch×8blk). 72 recorded self-play positions
(24 each opening / midgame / endgame) replayed from `epoch_000018–20` `.hxr` records.
Real native MCTS at 512 vs 1024 visits, **production config** (c_puct 1.5,
widening_max_children 96, widening_policy_mass 0.95, fpu 0.20, virtual_loss 1.0,
forced_playout_k 2.0, root_policy_temperature 1.1), but **Dirichlet OFF** and a
**fixed seed** so the only variable is the visit budget. `temperature=1.0` → the
visit_policy weights are visit fractions. CPU-only run (live trainer on GPU; zero
contention). Script: `analysis/_sim_resolution_test.py`.

## Numbers

| metric | overall | opening | midgame | endgame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **top-move agreement** | **77.8%** | 70.8% | 83.3% | 79.2% |
| visit KL(512‖1024) mean / med | 0.126 / 0.085 | 0.164 / 0.166 | 0.130 / 0.109 | 0.084 / 0.030 |
| Δ top-1 visit-fraction mean / med | 0.116 / 0.091 | 0.171 / 0.183 | 0.118 / 0.116 | 0.058 / 0.026 |
| **\|Δv\| root value** mean / med / max | **0.031 / 0.026 / 0.227** | 0.038 / 0.035 | 0.042 / 0.028 | 0.012 / 0.004 |
| 512 effective children (≥1% ≈ ≥5 visits) mean / med | 11.6 / 10 | 8.4 / 7 | 8.0 / 8 | 18.5 / 19 |
| 512 breadth (any visits) | 48.8 | 38.5 | 39.3 | 68.8 |
| 512 top-1 visit fraction | 0.376 | 0.407 | 0.519 | 0.202 |

**Disagreements:** 16 / 72 (22.2%). Of those, **9 are near-ties** (top-2 visit-fraction
margin < 0.03 at 512 — the search is genuinely indifferent); only **~7 / 72 ≈ 10%** are
non-trivial move flips. Value: \|Δv\| p90 = 0.059, one outlier 0.227. KL p90 = 0.286.

## Verdict — qualified: 512 is good for *value/play*, not fully converged for *policy targets*

Against the "good enough" bar (high agreement, small visit/value shift, well-concentrated):

- **Value: converged ✅.** \|Δv\| ~0.03 on [-1,1]; 1024 evaluates positions essentially
  the same as 512.
- **Concentration: healthy ✅.** Despite widening to 96, 512 puts meaningful visits on
  only ~8–12 children (≈50+ visits each on the leaders) — *not* spread thin.
- **Top-move agreement: moderate, not high ⚠️.** 77.8% — doubling sims flips the move
  ~22% of the time. But ~60% of flips are near-ties (low consequence); real decision
  changes ≈ 10%.
- **Policy-target shift: moderate ⚠️.** Visit distribution moves KL≈0.085 / top-1 ±0.1,
  concentrated in **opening/midgame** (opening agreement only 70.8%, KL 0.17). Endgame
  is nearly settled (KL med 0.03, \|Δv\| 0.012) — disagreements there are among
  near-equal moves that don't matter.

**Bottom line:** **512 is adequate for evaluation strength and play** (converged value,
well-concentrated search, most move-changes are near-ties), **but it is *not* fully
converged** — 1024 measurably sharpens/shifts the policy target and changes ~10% of
non-trivial decisions, almost entirely in the **opening and midgame**. If policy-target
quality matters (it does for training), there's a real case to raise sims for the
opening/midgame; if the run is purely compute-bound, 512 is a defensible operating point.
The cleanest cheap win would be **phase-dependent sims** (more in opening/midgame, fewer
in the already-settled endgame) rather than a flat 2× everywhere.

## Caveats
- Frozen epoch-20 net; agreement likely *rises* as priors sharpen at later epochs —
  worth re-running at a higher checkpoint before committing to a change.
- `forced_playout_k=2.0` is ON, which inflates low-visit children; the returned
  visit_policy may include forced playouts (KataGo prunes them from the *training*
  target), so "effective children"/KL slightly **overstate** spread vs the real target.
- Dirichlet was OFF to isolate the sim variable. Real self-play adds root Dirichlet
  noise, which independently perturbs move choice — so in live self-play the 512-vs-1024
  gap is partly washed out by noise.
