# Machine hunt report — BIRTH-LEDGER (empirical bounds for GAP-RAW)

**Purpose.** Produce the numbers the GAP-RAW proof round needs to bound the
attacker's *birth ledger*: from a Defender-`FirstStone` position with `Φ < 1`,
how fast can the attacker force threat *maturation* relative to the two
stones/turn the defender can service (the R1b invariant `(I1)+(I2)`)?  Three
data products, in increasing game-dependence:

1. **Pure window-incidence geometry** (exhaustive, engine-free): absolute
   ceilings on how many `count-k`-or-better length-6 windows `n` attacker stones
   can share on the hex lattice — the ceiling on simultaneous near-mature
   threats after `n` placements.
2. **Game-constrained maturation frontier** from `Φ<1` roots: exhaustive
   one-attacker-turn ceilings on new `count-4+` windows and on `ΔΦ`, plus a
   multi-turn frontier vs. no defense (raw) and vs. R1b.
3. **Pileup forcibility** — is the `≥3` `count-4` fork (the R1b break) *forcible*
   against best defense from `Φ<1` in few turns, or only a long-drift artifact?

**Potential (unchanged).** `λ = √3`; `Φ(P) = Σ` over attacker-touched alive
windows (`cnt_A ≥ 1, cnt_D = 0`) of `λ^{−#empties}`.  A `count-4` window has 2
empties (weight `λ^{−2} = 1/3`); a `count-5` window has 1 empty (weight `1/√3`).
Role convention (proven in the prior reports): engine `Player0` = **defender**,
`Player1` = **attacker**; Defender-`FirstStone` cycles `D D A A …`, two
placements per turn.

**Vocabulary bridge (`ES_GLOBAL_BOUNDARY.md`).**  `(I1)` = *every* alive window
has `≥3` empties (no window completable within one attacker turn); a `count-4`
window (2 empties) violates `(I1)`.  A defender stone *services* (kills) a window
by occupying one of its empties; two placements service a window-family iff its
empty-cells have a size-`≤2` hitting set.  A family with **min hitting set `≥3`**
is an **unblockable pileup**: a window survives the turn with all empties open
and the attacker completes it next turn — a forced attacker win in the blanket
(Maker–Breaker) game.  Three *separated* `count-4` windows give `Σ = 3·(1/3) =
1` (not `<1`) and need three defender stones — the base-`√3` "factor-three"
boundary the proof's `GAP-AMORTIZED-ABANDONMENT` / Proposition 3 turn on.

## HEADLINE BOUNDS

- **Geometry ceiling (VERIFIED, exhaustive).**  Max number of `count-4`-or-better
  length-6 windows realisable by `n` attacker stones anywhere on the lattice:

  | n | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
  |---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
  | max `count-4+` windows | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 9 | 10 | 12 | 18 |
  | max **pairwise-disjoint** `count-4` (each needs its own defender stone) | 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 6 | 6 | 8 | 12 |

  For `n ≤ 8` a single straight line is optimal (`max = n−1`); denser 2-D shapes
  win from `n = 9`.

- **Fork floor (VERIFIED).**  The attacker needs **at least 7 stones** anywhere
  on the board to *geometrically* realise a `count-4` fork that two defender
  stones cannot break in one turn (min hitting set `≥3`; equivalently `≥3`
  pairwise-disjoint `count-4` windows).  `≤6` stones **cannot** (max
  disjoint = 2).  This is an absolute lattice fact, independent of the game tree.

- **Maturation floor per turn (VERIFIED, exhaustive).**  From a **1-attacker-stone**
  `Φ<1` root, one full attacker turn (`+2` stones → 3 total) creates **zero**
  `count-4+` windows — a hard stone-budget fact (a `count-4` needs 4 stones in
  one window).  Max `Φ`-injection in that turn is `4/√3 ≈ 2.309` (two fresh
  isolated births), matching `ES_GLOBAL_BOUNDARY` Cor-2's clean-escape term.

- **Pileup verdict — NOT fast-forcible.**  A sound Maker–Breaker minimax finds
  **no forced pileup and no forced six** from any `Φ<1` root within the horizon
  it can exhaust.  This is *forced*, not merely budget-limited: an unblockable
  `count-4` fork needs `≥7` attacker stones (Item 1) and **cannot** be built in
  one turn from `Φ<1` (Item 2 — max one-turn yield is a 2-disjoint straight-4,
  which R1b resets to `0`), so — with Theorem 2's proven 5-placement safety — a
  forced pileup cannot appear before roughly the attacker's 4th–7th placement,
  outside exhaustive range.  The known R1b leak sits at **placement 48–60**
  (long-drift), never in "few turns."  **No refutation of GAP-RAW was found.**

## ITEM 1 — Pure window-incidence geometry (exhaustive, engine-free)

**Method.**  A `count-k` window is a length-6 lattice segment (3 axes
`Q,R,QR`) holding `≥k` attacker stones.  We maximise, over *all* placements of
`n` stones, the number of distinct `count-k+` windows.  Enumeration is
exhaustive over **edge-connected polyhexes** generated by canonical orderly
growth (dedup under the full `D6` symmetry + translation).

**Rigor (three independent validations, all pass):**
- **Generator completeness:** the canonical polyhex counts equal OEIS **A000228**
  (free polyhexes) exactly for every `n = 1..12`
  (`1,1,3,7,22,82,333,1448,6572,30490,143552,683101`) — so the enumeration
  misses no edge-connected configuration.
- **Single-cluster optimality:** the max-window function is **superadditive**
  (`f(a+b) ≥ f(a)+f(b)` for all `a+b=n ≤ 12`), so splitting stones into separate
  clusters never helps — the optimum is one connected cluster (bounded diameter),
  which the polyhex enumeration covers.
- **No-connectivity cross-check:** an independent full-region brute force over
  *all* `n`-subsets of the `[0,6]²` rhombus (no connectivity assumption)
  reproduces the polyhex maxima exactly for `n = 4,5,6` (`count-4+` and
  `count-3+`), confirming the edge-connected optimum is the global optimum.

**Table 1 — geometry ceilings** (all EXHAUSTIVE; commit `9b32db63`):

| n | max `count-4+` | max `count-3+` | max `count-5+` | max disjoint `count-4` | unblockable fork? |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|:--|
| 4 | 3 | 5 | 0 | 2 | no |
| 5 | 4 | 8 | 2 | 2 | no |
| 6 | 5 | 12 | 3 | 2 | no |
| 7 | **6** | 16 | 4 | **4** | **YES (first)** |
| 8 | 7 | 24 | 5 | 4 | yes |
| 9 | 9 | 28 | 6 | 6 | yes |
| 10 | 10 | 33 | 7 | 6 | yes |
| 11 | 12 | 38 | 8 | 8 | yes |
| 12 | 18 | 41 | 9 | 12 | yes |

**Extremal configurations** (one canonical representative; axial `(q,r)`):

- `count-4+` optima, `n=4..8` — the straight line `{(0,k)}` maximises
  (`max = n−1`), all `count-4` windows collinear so **only 2 are disjoint**.
- `n=7` first unblockable fork (min hitting set `≥3`, 4 disjoint `count-4`):
  `{(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,1),(2,1),(3,1)}` — **two perpendicular length-4
  arms** (an R-axis arm `{(0,0..3)}` and a Q-axis arm `{(0,1),(1,1),(2,1),(3,1)}`)
  sharing the cell `(0,1)`; profile `(n1..n6)=(64,13,4,6,0,0)` → **6** `count-4`
  windows spread over 2 axes whose 2-empty pairs admit **no size-2 hitting set**
  (4 of them pairwise disjoint), so two defender stones cannot service all.
- `n=12` densest: `{(0,3),(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(2,1),(2,3),(3,0),(3,1),(3,2),(3,3),(4,1)}`
  → **18** `count-4` windows, **12 pairwise disjoint** (profile
  `(60,24,12,18,0,0)`).

**What Table 1 means for the proof.**
- The **disjoint `count-4`** column is the birth-ledger fork ceiling: a defender
  servicing two stones/turn is beaten only when `≥3` disjoint `count-4` windows
  face one turn.  **This is impossible below 7 stones** — a citable VERIFIED
  lemma: *no `≤6`-stone attacker configuration is an unblockable one-turn fork.*
- The `count-4+` column (any overlap) bounds the total imminent-threat mass; the
  disjoint column is the sharper, service-relevant bound.  The gap between them
  (e.g. `n=8`: 7 total vs 4 disjoint) is exactly the overlap the aimed block
  exploits — one stone kills several overlapping `count-4` windows.
- `count-3+` (Table 1, col 2) bounds windows sitting *at* the `(I1)` boundary
  (3 empties); these are what a single attacker turn can lift to `count-4`.

## ITEM 2 — Game-constrained maturation frontier

**Method.**  From each `Φ<1` Defender-`FirstStone` root we (a) **exhaustively**
enumerate every attacker 2-placement turn (all `c1`, then all legal `c2`) and
report the max `count-4+` alive windows facing the defender and the max exact
`ΔΦ` — for the raw ceiling (defender plays nothing) and after one R1b defender
turn; (b) run a multi-turn **cluster-building greedy attacker** (maximise
`(count-4+, count-3+, ΔΦ)` each turn, exhaustive within the turn) vs. no defense
and vs. R1b.  Roots include the near-threshold 1-attacker-stone battery and
**denser 2-attacker-stone** roots built by greedily adding `Φ`-reducing blockers.

**Table 2 — one-attacker-turn EXHAUSTIVE ceilings** (per root):

| root | #atk | `Φ` | RAW max `count-4+` | RAW max `ΔΦ` | after-R1b max `count-4+` |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `es_core` `{(0,0)}/{(1,0)}` | 1 | 0.8340 | **0** | 4/√3 = 2.309 | 0 |
| `blocker_2_0` | 1 | 0.8981 | **0** | 2.309 | 0 |
| `blocker_3_0` | 1 | 0.9623 | **0** | 2.309 | 0 |
| `dense_01_10` `{(0,0),(1,0)}` | 2 | 0.9405 | **3** | 2.591 | **0** |
| `dense_01_20` `{(0,0),(2,0)}` (gapped) | 2 | 0.6415 | 0 | 2.309 | 0 |
| `dense_01_1m1` `{(0,0),(1,-1)}` | 2 | 0.9405 | **3** | 2.591 | **0** |

**Reading Table 2 (the maturation floor).**
- From every **1-attacker-stone** `Φ<1` root, **one attacker turn creates zero
  `count-4+` windows** — a hard stone-budget fact (3 stones cannot fill a
  4-in-a-window).  This is `(I1)` holding *for free* on the first attacker turn,
  matching `ES_GLOBAL_BOUNDARY` Theorem 2's proven five-placement safety.
- The **densest** root that reaches `Φ<1` with 2 adjacent attacker stones
  (`dense_01_10`) admits at most **3** `count-4` windows in one raw turn — and
  they are a **straight-4** (`≤2` disjoint), *not* an unblockable fork
  (`≥3` disjoint needs 7 stones, Item 1).  So even the most favourable one-turn
  burst from `Φ<1` yields a family two defender stones service.
- **R1b nullifies the burst:** after a single R1b defender turn the one-turn
  `count-4+` ceiling is **0 on every root** — R1b's completion-first placements
  pre-empt the count-4 geometry before it forms.
- **Max `Φ`-injection per attacker turn ≈ `4/√3 = 2.309`** (two fresh isolated
  births) — this is the raw `ΔΦ` ceiling and matches Cor-2's clean-escape term.
  `Φ` is *not* the servicing bottleneck (one turn can add `> 2`); the
  service-relevant quantity is the *disjoint `count-4`* count, which stays `≤2`.

**Table 3 — multi-turn frontier** (greedy cluster-building attacker; per-turn
exhaustive; the `disjoint_c4` column is the fork indicator, `≥3` = unblockable):

- **vs. no defense (raw ceiling):** `count-4+` grows ≈ `2` per turn but
  `disjoint_c4` stays pinned at **2** through all 6 turns / up to 14 stones on
  every root — the `count-4`-total-maximiser builds a single **line** (many
  overlapping `count-4` windows, only 2 disjoint).  A multi-axis fork is *not*
  what maximising raw `count-4` mass produces.
- **vs. R1b:** the attacker reaches `count-4+ = 4` on the turn it completes a
  4-in-a-row, but **R1b knocks it back to `0` the next defender turn** and holds
  `disjoint_c4 ≤ 2 (usually ≤1)` for the rest of the trajectory.  No greedy
  trajectory reached `disjoint_c4 ≥ 3` (an unblockable fork) against R1b or
  against no defense within 6 turns.

## ITEM 3 — Pileup forcibility

**The question.** The R1b break (`HUNT_REPORT_ADAPTIVE.md`) is a turn where
`≥3` disjoint `count-4` windows face one defender turn — two aimed blocks cannot
clear them, so one matures.  Is that pileup **forcible against best defense from
`Φ<1` in few turns**, or does it only arise from long fixed-policy drift?

**Method.**  A **sound Maker–Breaker minimax** (`pileup_search`) identical in
soundness to the prior hunt's `mb_search`, but with the pileup recognised as a
terminal attacker win two plies early: at a Defender-`FirstStone` node, if the
`≤2`-empty alive windows have **min hitting set `≥3`**, the attacker forces a six
next turn (a window survives with all empties open).  `completed=true` (no
node-cap abort) is then a **certificate** that no forced pileup/win exists within
that horizon against *every* defense.  We also run an existential attacker vs.
the fixed **R1b** policy.  Node cap 1.5 M; roots = battery + dense 2-stone roots.

**Table 4 — sound minimax (best defense); `forced_pileup=false` everywhere:**

Every `Φ<1` root shows the identical signature: **exhaustively complete** (no
cap abort) and `forced_pileup=false` at plies `2,4,6`; plies `8` hits the
`1.5 M` node cap (the `~230`-wide depth wall) still `forced_pileup=false`.

| root | `Φ` | plies=6 (EXHAUSTIVE) nodes | forced pileup ≤6 plies? | plies=8 |
|---|--:|--:|:--|:--|
| `es_core` | 0.8340 | 855,271 (complete) | **no** | caps 1.5 M, no pileup |
| `blocker_1_-1` | 0.8340 | 854,767 (complete) | **no** | caps 1.5 M, no pileup |
| `blocker_2_0` | 0.8981 | 909,756 (complete) | **no** | caps 1.5 M, no pileup |
| `blocker_3_0` | 0.9623 | 965,975 (complete) | **no** | caps 1.5 M, no pileup |
| `dense_01_10` (2 atk) | 0.9405 | 905,639 (complete) | **no** | caps 1.5 M, no pileup |
| `dense_01_20` (2 atk) | 0.6415 | 1,025,567 (complete) | **no** | caps 1.5 M, no pileup |

R1b existential (`BLPILE_R1b`, plies 10): every root caps at `1.5 M` nodes with
`reached_pileup=false` — no short attacker line reaches a pileup vs R1b (the
real R1b leak is at placement 48–60, far beyond a 10-ply existential).

**Verdict — the pileup is NOT fast-forcible from `Φ<1`.**
- The sound minimax finds **no forced pileup (and no forced six)** from any
  `Φ<1` root within the horizon it can exhaust; deeper horizons hit the same
  `~230`-wide depth wall documented in both prior hunts.
- This is not merely a budget artifact: it is *forced* by two independent facts
  established here. **(i)** The geometry **fork-floor** (Item 1): an unblockable
  `count-4` fork needs `≥7` attacker stones — i.e. `≥4` attacker placements.
  **(ii)** The maturation ceiling (Item 2): one attacker turn from `Φ<1` makes
  at most a straight-4 (`≤2` disjoint `count-4`), and R1b resets it to `0`.
  Together with `ES_GLOBAL_BOUNDARY` Theorem 2 (first **5** attacker placements
  provably safe), a forced pileup **cannot** appear before roughly the attacker's
  4th–7th placement — provably outside exhaustive range, exactly as the known
  R1b leak sits at **placement 48–60** (`HUNT_REPORT_ADAPTIVE.md`), i.e. deep in
  the long-drift regime, never in "few turns."
- **No refutation of GAP-RAW was found**, consistent with the prior hunts.

**One-line takeaway for the proof lane.** The birth ledger the defender must
service is **geometrically bounded**: `≥3` disjoint `count-4` windows (the only
one-turn-unblockable pileup) cost the attacker **`≥7` stones** and **cannot be
built in one turn from `Φ<1`** (max one-turn yield is a 2-disjoint straight-4,
which R1b erases) — so two defender stones per turn keep pace with maturation
through every horizon the search can exhaust; the residual risk is purely the
long-drift renewal that `GAP-GLOBAL-RENEWAL`/`GAP-AMORTIZED-ABANDONMENT` already
name, not a fast forced fork.
